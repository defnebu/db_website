## About Me

I am a third year Ph.D. candidate at Brown University's Neuroscience Graduate Program and am advised by Dr. Theresa Desrochers. My research interests include testing the alignment between measures that reflect (spiking) or are thought to approximate neural activity (local field potential, cerebral blood volume) in the animal model. This line of work aims to facilitate more productive crosstalk between systems and cognitive neuroscience. I also work on understanding what representations in fronto-striatal circuits may support de novo sequence structure learning in primates. This line of work aims to identify what multi-area representations are useful for learning ordinal structure in the environment. 

Before enrolling in the program, I worked with Dr. David Badre and Dr. Apoorva Bhandari on characterizing how task structure shapes the geometry of neural representations in frontal cortex in humans with fMRI. 

Even before that, I earned my BA in Statistics at the University of Chicago where I designed experiments to try to understand multi-attribute value based decision making in Dr. Akram Bakkour's lab. It is here where I first understood that we can study, understand, and perturb complex behavior.
